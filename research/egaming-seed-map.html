Pick your lane before you pick your list

These are three different fundraising markets with barely overlapping investor sets. Being precise about which one you're in is the single biggest determinant of hit rate — the rows below are tagged accordingly.

LANE A

Regulated real-money

Licensed online casino, sportsbook, lottery, and the B2B stack underneath them.

Funded by: gambling-native funds and strategics. Effectively nobody else.
LANE B

Sweepstakes, social casino, skill-cash

Dual-currency sweeps, social slots, skill games for cash prizes. Where the 2026 deal energy is.

Funded by: a thin overlap of gambling-native, games-native and risk-tolerant consumer funds.
LANE C

F2P with casino mechanics

Casual and social slots, poker, bingo — casino-themed but no cash out.

Funded by: the games-native seed funds. The deepest and most accessible pool.

The gate is the LP agreement, not the thesis

Ask about vice clauses in the first email

Pension funds, endowments and sovereign wealth funds have historically barred gambling exposure in their LP agreements. VC firms rarely carry their own vice clause — their limited partners do. Many LPs dropped sports betting from those clauses after PASPA fell; casino is loosening more slowly. A partner can love the deck and still be structurally unable to invest, so ask early: "Do you have LP restrictions on real-money gaming?" It saves a month per firm.

Second-order effect worth planning around: for tier-one generalists in 2026, the phrases "prediction markets," "competitive entertainment" and "skill-based cash gaming" clear investment committees that "online casino" does not. Use the accurate description of what you're building — but know which door each one opens.

What they will underwrite

Sector investors ask for these by name. Have them in the deck, not in the follow-up.

  • Payer conversionSweeps and social casino investors index on this above almost everything else.
  • Retention, split two waysD1 / D7 / D30 overall, and payer retention reported separately.
  • ARPDAU and ARPPUPlus the whale curve — what share of revenue comes from your top 1%.
  • LTV : CAC and payback windowSub-six-month payback is the bar for anything UA-financed.
  • Contribution marginAgainst public comps. This is the number that made 2026 sweepstakes economics attractive.
  • Regulatory postureLicensing, geolocation stack, KYC/AML vendor, state-by-state exposure, and your plan for an adverse AG action.
  • Responsible gaming controlsNow a diligence line item, not a nice-to-have.
  • Payments redundancyProcessor mix and decline rates. Payment fragility kills more RMG startups than product does.

Non-dilutive capital is often the better instrument

Consumer casino is user-acquisition-bound, not R&D-bound. Selling 20% of the company to buy installs is the most expensive way to finance a paid-marketing engine. Sharp Alpha's $150M UA facility ($4M–$30M+, growth stage) is the sector-native option; mobile-gaming UA financiers such as Pollen VC and Braavo cover the smaller end. Model the effective APR against your payback window before you compare it to an equity round.

Suggested sequence

  1. Sector natives first. Sharp Alpha, Velo, Yolo, Bettor Capital, Acies. They move fastest and their terms set your price. In parallel, operator angels — the cheapest credibility you can buy, and often what unlocks the funds.
  2. Games-native funds on the lane-C framing: BITKRAFT, Makers Fund, Play Ventures, LVP, Sisu, Konvoy, The Games Fund, F4.
  3. Sports and betting-adjacent: SeventySix, Courtside, KB Partners. Good for consumer brands with a sports surface.
  4. Generalists, only with the vice-clause question asked upfront and a non-gambling framing that is still true. Then strategics for distribution — never as your only path, because they move on their own timeline.
